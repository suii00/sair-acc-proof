# Two conjugations for AK(1)

Claude Fable 5.2 supplied a folded-graph lower bound and an explicit upper bound. The free-word calculations and graph below were independently checked. The cost counts individual conjugations by arbitrary ambient words. Relator inversions and ordinary right multiplications have cost zero. These free moves generate the Nielsen transformations, including inverse products and left products.

## Lower bound

Write $a=xy^{-2}$, use the ambient basis $(a,y)$, and denote $A=a^{-1}$ and $Y=y^{-1}$. The original relator pair becomes $(a,ay^3aYAY)$. Its generated subgroup is $H=\langle a,b\rangle$, where $b=y^3aYAY=y dY$ and $d=y^2aYA$.

The subgroup graph based at vertex 0 has the following positive edges. Reverse traversal reads the inverse letter.

| Label | Directed edges |
|---|---|
| $a$ | $0\to0$, $3\to4$, $1\to5$ |
| $y$ | $0\to1$, $1\to2$, $2\to3$, $5\to4$ |

This connected graph is folded because each vertex has at most one incoming and one outgoing edge of each positive label. It consists of the $a$ loop, the bridge $0\to1$, and the cycle based at 1 labelled $d$. The fundamental group has free basis $a, y dY$. Foldedness makes its label map into the ambient free group injective.

In a cyclically reduced closed path that crosses the bridge, each direction of the bridge occurs, so the label contains both $y$ and $Y$. A nonempty cyclically reduced loop entirely in the other cycle is a power of $d$ up to rotation and also contains both signs of $y$. The only remaining possibility is a power of $a$. In rank two, a cyclically reduced ambient primitive word can use at most one sign of each generator. This follows from the characterization of primitive elements as conjugates of Christoffel words and their construction in each quadrant. Thus any ambient primitive element of $H$ has cyclic reduction $a^{\pm1}$ and in particular has $y$-exponent zero. [Kassel and Reutenauer, Corollary 3.3(b) and Remark 3.4(a)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0507219#page=17)

In a path with one conjugation, the pair immediately after that move is Nielsen equivalent to the standard pair, hence is an ambient basis. Both entries immediately before it would then be ambient primitive, because conjugation preserves primitivity. These entries still generate $H$, since the preceding Nielsen moves preserve the generated subgroup. Both would have $y$-exponent zero, although $b\in H$ has $y$-exponent one. A path with zero conjugations gives the same contradiction from the initial pair. This proves the lower bound of two.

## Upper bound

The first relator stays $a$ during the operations displayed below.

| Operation on the second relator | Result |
|---|---|
| Start | $ay^3aYAY$ |
| Left-multiply by $A$ | $y^3aYAY$ |
| Conjugate by $(y^3a)^{-1}$ | $YAy^2a$ |
| Right-multiply by $A$ | $YAy^2$ |
| Conjugate by $y$ | $Ay$ |
| Left-multiply by $a$ | $y$ |

Two ordinary right multiplications of the first relator by the second finish at $(ay^2,y)=(x,y)$. Direct free-word evaluation in the original generators verified a replay of 15 official moves. In order, write $I_i$ for inversion, $M_{ij}$ for $r_i\gets r_i r_j$, and $C_i(g)$ for $r_i\gets gr_ig^{-1}$, with slots numbered 0 and 1. The replay is

$$ I_1 M_{10} I_1,\quad C_1((y^3a)^{-1}),\quad
I_0 M_{10} I_0,\quad C_1(y),\quad
I_1 I_0 M_{10} I_1 I_0,\quad M_{01} M_{01}. $$

The two displayed conjugations attain the lower bound. The length 15 describes this replay only.

## Independent review of the general lower bound

A second Claude Fable 5.2 review accepted the following uniform argument. For $n\ge1$, set $m=n(n+1)+2$ and work in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)/\{\pm I\}$. Take $X=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $Y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Already over the integers $XYX=YXY$, and $X^nY^{-(n+1)}$ has determinant one and trace $m$. Its square modulo $m$ is $-I$ by the two-by-two characteristic identity, which holds over every commutative ring. Thus the projected starting pair lies in a cyclic subgroup of order at most two.

Nielsen moves keep the entries in this subgroup until the first conjugation. Immediately after one conjugation, each entry squares to the identity. The subgroup they generate is a quotient of the infinite dihedral group, in which all squares commute. All further conjugation-free steps stay inside that subgroup. At the target the squares $X^2$ and $(XY)^2$ fail to commute. The lower right entries of their products in the two orders are respectively 0 and $-2$. They differ even up to overall sign modulo every $m\ge4$. This proves the two-conjugation obstruction regardless of the size of the subgroup generated by $X,Y$.

Direct modular arithmetic also checked the displayed relations for every $1\le n\le256$. To add this obstruction to other lower bounds, the same path must have at least $n+2$ individual right multiplications and at least two individual inversions. Then the three disjoint constructor counts give at least $n+6$ moves. A bound on aggregate nonmultiplication moves would not suffice for that addition.

## Independent review of the exact nine-move distance

Claude Fable 5.2 accepted the exact directed distance from $AK(1)$ to the ordered pair $(x,y)$. With $X=x^{-1}$ and $Y=y^{-1}$, it replayed

$$ I_0,\ M_{10},\ C_1(YX),\ M_{10},\ C_1(xyX),\ I_0,\ M_{10},\ M_{01},\ M_{01}. $$

The first conjugation leaves $r_1=xYXyy$, and the second gives $r_1=yyyX$. The last three moves pass through $(xYY,y)$ and $(xY,y)$ to reach $(x,y)$. Fable confirmed the simultaneous lower bounds of five right multiplications, two inversions, and two arbitrary-word conjugations. In the $S_4$ argument, each entry immediately after a single conjugation is the identity or a transposition. Two disjoint transpositions generate a Klein four-group, and two distinct transpositions meeting in one point generate an $S_3$. Neither contains a four-cycle. The multiplication argument uses the cycle type of each entry, which survives subsequent conjugations. The parity of the binary exponent matrix forces an odd multiplication count, while its integral determinant and initial negative $y$-column force at least two inversions.
