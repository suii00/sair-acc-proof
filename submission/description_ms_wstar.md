# Three conjugations are necessary throughout the Miller–Schupp w-star family and sufficient at n=1

Let w* = y⁻¹xyx⁻¹. For each n ≥ 1, let c(n) denote the minimum number of arbitrary-word conjugations in an ordinary Andrews–Curtis path from the ordered Miller–Schupp pair MS(n,w*) to (x,y). Then

    3 ≤ c(n) ≤ 2^n + 1,     c(1) = 3.

At n=1 a ten-move path attains this minimum; ten is the shortest length among paths with three conjugations. At n=2 the bounds give 3 ≤ c(2) ≤ 5. If the sum of the freely reduced row lengths must stay at its initial value or below, the minimum at n=2 is exactly five. The exact unrestricted counts for n ≥ 2 remain open here.

Shehper et al. established ordinary AC-triviality of this entire family in Theorem B(ii), proved as Theorem 3. They also credit earlier automated proofs for n=3 through 8 to Lisitsa. We measure the cost of trivializations and give certificates in the ordered SAIR move system. Uppercase X and Y denote the inverses of x and y. The starting rows are R = X y^n x Y^(n+1) and S = xxYXy. I_i inverts row i, M_i multiplies it on the right by the other row, and C_i(g) replaces it by g r_i g⁻¹. An entire word g costs one conjugation. Row indices start at zero. [Shehper et al., Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15332v2#S3.SS4)

### Why different conjugations are necessary

The two exponent-sum rows always form an ordered basis of the two-dimensional vector space over F₂. Initially they are (0,1) and (1,0). Inversion and conjugation leave them unchanged modulo two; right multiplication adds the other row. Exactly one of the three nonzero linear functionals takes the value 1 on both members of any such basis.

For each chosen functional, take a group evaluation having that sign character, in which the starting rows lie in a common subgroup of order two generated by a sign-odd element, while the target rows generate a noncyclic subgroup. Follow this evaluation up to the first time the rows cease to lie in such a two-element subgroup. Inversions and right multiplications preserve containment. If one row is even, it is the identity in the current subgroup. Conjugating either row then leaves a pair in a subgroup of the same kind, since a conjugate of an odd involution is again an odd involution. Thus a first departure requires a conjugation with both rows odd. Distinct functionals require distinct steps. This counts first departures and allows later returns.

Two evaluations work for every n ≥ 1. Write the dihedral group of order 2m with rotation r of order m and reflection s, where srs = r⁻¹.

* For the x-parity functional take m=2n+1 and (x,y)=(s,r). Then R=r^(-(2n+1))=1 and S=r⁻¹sr is a reflection.
* For y-parity take m=3 and (x,y)=(r,s). Then S=r³=1. For even n one has R=s; for odd n one has R=r⁻¹sr.

In both cases the images of the target generate a noncyclic dihedral group. For odd n the third functional, x-parity plus y-parity, is the sign on S₃ with x=(12), y=(23). The starting rows evaluate to the same transposition (13), and x,y generate S₃. In particular all three evaluations are copies of S₃ when n=1.

For even n ≥ 4 put m=n−1 and work over K=(ℤ/mℤ)[i]/(i²+1). Define a=(i−1)/2, b=i−1/2, and take the following determinant-one matrices:

    P = [[a,b],[i,a]],     Q = [[1,1],[0,1]].

Since Q^m=I, the images of both rows under (x,y)=(P,Q) are

    P⁻¹ Q P Q⁻² = P² Q⁻¹ P⁻¹ Q = E = [[−a,i/2−1],[1,a]].

The trace is zero, so E²=−I, and its lower-left entry is 1. In SL₂(K)/{±I} it is a nonidentity involution. PQ and QP have equal lower-left entry i and respective upper-left entries a and a+i. They differ even up to sign since i and 2i are nonzero. Append a C₂ factor with both generators odd. Both starting rows now equal the same odd involution, and the target images fail to commute. This supplies the third functional over composite m as well.

### The exceptional calculation at n=2

Use the two dihedral evaluations above and two evaluations into S₅. In one-line notation on 0,1,2,3,4, with products acting rightmost first, the additional pairs (x,y) are (01243,12340) and (02143,13402). In each factor identify ordered pairs up to simultaneous conjugation. A zero-cost step performs the same inversion or positive right multiplication in every factor. A unit-cost step chooses the same row in every factor and permits every conjugate of that row independently in each factor. Every original path projects to this finite graph.

Take the zero-cost closure of each endpoint. From each closure insert zero or one conjugation and close again at zero cost. Reverse reachability uses the same closures, since each zero-cost generator is a permutation of a finite set and conjugation edges are reversible. The start and target zero-cost closures have 6 and 216 states. Their respective at-most-one-conjugation closures have 540 and 18,372 states, with empty intersection. Any path using at most two conjugations would give a point in the intersection. This excludes two without imposing a bound on word length, conjugator length or total moves.

### The sharp case n=1

The following is the full free reduction in chronological order.

```text
move      row 0   row 1
start     XyxYY   xxYXy
I0        yyXYx   xxYXy
C0(Yx)    YxyyX   xxYXy
M1        YxyyX   xxyX
I0        xYYXy   xxyX
C1(YX)    xYYXy   Yxyy
M0        x       Yxyy
I0        X       Yxyy
C1(Xy)    X       yx
M1        X       y
I0        x       y
```

The peak total length is 10, the length of the starting pair. To test shortestness with exactly three conjugations, enumerate all sequences of at most six inversions and right multiplications taking the initial integer exponent matrix to the identity. Insert the three conjugations in every choice of positions and rows, including consecutive moves. Through total length nine this yields 1,792 templates, all of length eight. Evaluate each prefix and reversed suffix about the middle conjugation in a finite permutation group. At the join the untouched row must agree and the changed rows must be conjugate in the subgroup generated by x,y. For (x,y)=(234510,214503) in S₆, 72 templates survive; (1230,1302) in S₄ removes all 72. No conjugating word has been bounded. The same exponent enumeration requires at least five nonconjugating moves. With four or more conjugations the length is therefore at least nine. The overall minimum is either nine or ten.

### An explicit path for every n

Write m=2^n−1. Invert S and keep S⁻¹ as the second row during the following substitutions. Replace occurrences of Xy by yXX in the first row, moving the X letters across one y at a time from right to left within each batch. Replacing A Xy B by A yXX B right-multiplies the word by B⁻¹ S⁻¹ B. If the first row currently has the form h R' h⁻¹, conjugate it by B h⁻¹ and multiply on the right by S⁻¹. The result is B times the rewritten first row times B⁻¹. After j batches the unframed word is y^j X^(2^j) y^(n−j) x Y^(n+1). The total number of substitutions is m.

After the last substitution the actual first row freely reduces to x^(2−m) Y X². Restore S by inversion and conjugate the first row by x^(m−2), obtaining A=Y X^m. Multiply the second row by A and conjugate it by X² to obtain B=Y X^(m−1). Since A⁻¹B=x, an inversion and a right multiplication give (x,B). Multiply the second row on the right by x a further m−1 times and invert it. This gives (x,y) using m+2 conjugations and 3m+7 moves.

For n=2 this path peaks at 12. A separate exact search at cap 12 gives minimum conjugation cost five. Its vertices are all reachable ordered pairs with freely reduced total length at most 12. Edges have costs zero for I/M and one for each conjugation. To enumerate all bounded endpoints of a conjugation, cyclically reduce that row, take every rotation of its core, and wrap it in all reduced inverse pairs that fit the cap. The unconstrained minimum c(2) could be smaller if longer intermediate words are used.

### Verification

Hideto Iitsuka's ACC-P000035 treated the Akbulut–Kirby pair AK(2). We use the same ordinary-move counting conventions, marked-image obstruction method and finite move-template test. The three parity functionals, the Miller–Schupp evaluations and the paths here concern a different family. [ACC-P000035](https://competition.sair.foundation/contributor-network/acc/ACC-P000035)

The Lean theorem `AC.MSStar_one_conjCount_exact` gives the exact minimum at n=1 in the official definitions. `AC.MSStar_one_witness` checks the ten moves and `AC.MSStarThree.bounds` gives the lower bounds of three for odd n and two for positive even n. As a boundary check, `AC.MSStar_zero_conjCount_exact` gives c(0)=1 when the definition is extended to zero. The stronger positive even-index lower bound uses the matrix proof and the separate finite certificate above. The repository contains Python checks for the finite groups, the move counts, the peak-constrained graph, and the uniform path generator. [Official Lean move definitions](https://github.com/SAIRcompetition/Andrews-Curtis/blob/a0fd6e6f52d82c93ccc06ab91d81e8fa3678256e/competition/tools/lean/AC.lean)

Codex developed the certificates above. In Open Math OS, Kimi K3.1 Pro with Ultramath independently replayed a three-conjugation path at n=1 and found an independent S₆ obstruction excluding two conjugations for every n congruent to 3 modulo 6.
