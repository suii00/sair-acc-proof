# Exactly three conjugations suffice and are necessary for AK(2)

For the ordered pair

    AK(n) = (x^n y^(-(n+1)), xyx (yxy)^(-1))

in the free group F(x,y), the minimum number of conjugations taking AK(2) to the ordered pair (x,y) is exactly **three**. This minimum allows any number of inversions and right multiplications and arbitrary conjugating words. More generally, every ordinary path from AK(n) to (x,y), for n ≥ 2, uses at least three conjugations and at least n+7 moves. The assertion for general n is conditional on the existence of such a path.

### A witness in the free group

Use zero-based row indices. I_i inverts row i, M_i right-multiplies it by the other row, and C_i(w) replaces it by w r_i w^(-1). Uppercase X,Y denote x^(-1),y^(-1). The following ledger gives every freely reduced intermediate pair.

```text
step   move       row 0         row 1
 0     start      xxYYY         xyxYXY
 1     I0         yyyXX         xyxYXY
 2     M1         yyyXX         xyxYXyyXX
 3     C1(xYX)    yyyXX         xxYXyyXyX
 4     M0         yyXyyXyX      xxYXyyXyX
 5     I1         yyXyyXyX      xYxYYxyXX
 6     C1(xyXX)   yyXyyXyX      xyXYxYY
 7     M1         yyXyyXyX      xyXyXyX
 8     C0(xY)     xyXyyXyXyX    xyXyXyX
 9     I1         xyXyyXyXyX    xYxYxYX
10     M0         xyXyX         xYxYxYX
11     M1         xyXyX         xY
12     M0         xyX           xY
13     M0         x             xY
14     I1         x             yX
15     M1         x             y
```

The conjugations are steps 3, 6 and 8. The ledger uses eight right products and four inversions. Its peak total relator length is 17. The following shorter path peaks at 23.

```text
I0 M1 C1(xYX) M0 C1(yyXyyXX) M1 C1(xYxYY) I0 M1 M0 M0 M1 M0
```

### Why two conjugations cannot suffice

The following two evaluations into S₄ make the AK(2) lower bound hand-checkable. Permutations act rightmost first. Under the marking A given by x=(1234), y=(1243), the two initial relators map to ((14),1). Under B given by x=(12), y=(134), they map to (1,(14)). In A, the squares of x and xY are (13)(24) and (134). In B, the squares of xY and y are (13)(24) and (143). In each case the displayed squares do not commute.

Before the first conjugation, the two rows in the product of the evaluations form a basis of the initial Klein four group. Inversion and right multiplication preserve its generated subgroup. In additive binary coordinates, the two basis vectors cannot both equal (1,1). Thus in at least one coordinate one row is the identity. Whichever row is conjugated first, that coordinate still generates at most one involution. Up to the second conjugation both rows stay in that cyclic subgroup. After the second, they belong to a subgroup generated by two involutions, and any remaining inversions and right multiplications stay inside it. In a group generated by two involutions s,t, every element has the form (st)^k or (st)^k s. Its square belongs to the cyclic subgroup generated by st. Its squares therefore commute. Both possible target evaluations violate this condition. The same reasoning excludes zero or one conjugation.

### Uniform extension

For the first factor, use the marking from ACC-P000033 over Z/q, where q=n(n+1)+2. Take the determinant-one matrices A=[[1,1],[0,1]], B=[[1,0],[-1,1]] modulo simultaneous sign. They satisfy ABA=BAB. The first relator has determinant one and trace zero modulo q, so it squares to -I. Its upper-right entry n makes its projective image nontrivial. The lower-right entries of A²(AB)² and (AB)²A² are 0 and -2, which distinguish the products even modulo sign.

For a complementary factor let m be the odd member of {n,n+1}. Here m ≥ 3. Over Z/m put U=[[0,1],[1,0]] and V=[[1,1],[0,1]]. Choose (x,y)=(U,V) for even n and (V,U) for odd n. Since U²=V^m=I, the first relator maps to I. The second maps to D=[[-1,2],[0,1]], a nontrivial involution. Swapping U,V replaces D by D^(-1)=D. The upper-left entries of V²(UV)² and (UV)²V² are 3 and 1. Their difference survives modulo m. The same Klein four argument now applies to every n ≥ 2. ACC-P000032 and ACC-P000033 supply the independent cost of at least n+4 nonconjugation moves, yielding n+7 in total.

### Shortest length and peak at fixed conjugation cost

**Length at minimum conjugation count.** Among paths using exactly three conjugations, the minimum total move count is 13. For the lower bound, encode a trace by its row and operation at each step, leaving each conjugator unspecified. Conjugations preserve the matrix of exponent sums. Enumerate all sequences of the other four operations of length at most 9 taking the initial matrix ((2,-3),(1,-1)) to the identity and insert exactly three conjugations in all positions and on either row. Exactly 4,032 templates have length 9 and 195,360 have length 11. These are the only lengths through 12 that survive the exponent test. Filter the templates with three permutation markings. In one-line notation on 0,1,2,3 the successive (x,y) are (1230,1302), (0132,1203), (1203,0213). For each template propagate the possible pairs, allowing every conjugator in the subgroup generated by that marking. The surviving counts at lengths (9,11) are successively (174,9456), (24,1296), and (0,0). Any free-group trace would survive all three tests. Thus 13 is shortest **subject to three conjugations**. The overall shortest length when more conjugations are allowed remains open here.

**Intermediate length.** Measure the peak by the maximum of |r₀|+|r₁| over all visited pairs, including the endpoints. The entire list of nondominated (conjugation count, peak) pairs is **(3,17), (4,15), (5,13)**. The first ledger realizes (3,17). The following two paths realize the remaining pairs.

```text
(4,15)  I0 M1 C0(X) C1(YX) M0 I0 C0(yXy) M1 C0(xY) I1 M0 M1 M1 I1 M0 M0
(5,13)  I1 C1(xYX) M1 I0 C0(Y) M1 C0(Xy) M0 I0 C0(Yx) M1 C0(xY) I0 M0 M1 I1 I0 M1 M0
```

For each cap B, the vertices are ordered pairs of freely reduced words of total length ≤B. Zero-cost edges are inversion and positive right multiplication. For a unit-cost edge, retain the untouched row and enumerate the possible replacements for the conjugated row by taking every rotation of its cyclically reduced core and wrapping it in each sequence of inverse letter pairs that keeps the word freely reduced and within B. This enumerates every permitted endpoint of an arbitrary-word conjugation. Immediately after the last conjugation, the state must lie in the reverse zero-cost closure of (x,y). A final conjugation joins two states precisely when they agree in the untouched row and have equal cyclic cores up to rotation in the changed row. Breadth-first layers in the number of conjugations give the minimum costs for caps 12,13,14,15,16,17 as unreachable,5,5,4,4,3. At cap 12 there are exactly 1,340 reachable states. An independent forward enumeration using signed unordered rows and single-letter generation of the bounded conjugacy classes reproduces all six outcomes. Monotonicity in B, the explicit paths and the unrestricted lower bound of three give the stated frontier. In particular the 13-move and peak-17 assertions minimize two different quantities.

### Prior work and verification

AK(2) was already known to admit an ordinary trivialization. Havas and Ramsay compute 14 essential moves under their single-letter conjugation and endpoint conventions. The quantity optimized here charges one for a conjugation by an entire word and zero for each other move. Hideto Iitsuka's ACC-P000033 establishes a lower bound of two conjugations for n ≥ 1 and attains it at AK(1). Here two complementary markings exclude two for n ≥ 2, and the word ledger realizes the new lower bound at n=2. The question of equality at n ≥ 3 remains open in this work. [Havas–Ramsay, §§1–3](https://staff.itee.uq.edu.au/havas/2003hr.pdf) · [ACC-P000032](https://competition.sair.foundation/contributor-network/acc/ACC-P000032) · [ACC-P000033](https://competition.sair.foundation/contributor-network/acc/ACC-P000033)

Korea Superintelligence Labs previously reported an exact peak of 13 for its elementary move system without a conjugation budget. Here the three separate optima control the number of arbitrary-word conjugations and use the ordered SAIR endpoint.
